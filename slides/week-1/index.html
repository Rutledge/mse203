Part two

Why AI products
are different

The core shift

Traditional software

Same input, same output.

You can test it exhaustively, and a failure means there's a bug to find.

"Correct" is well defined.

AI products

The same input can give a different answer each time.

You can't enumerate the cases, and a failure often isn't traceable to any one bug.

"Correct" is frequently a judgment call.

The tradeoffs that matter right now
Non-determinism — the same input can give you a different answer twice in a row.
Latency — sometimes a fast okay answer beats a slow great one.
Quality — often subjective, and hard to even define.
Cost — you pay per call, and it adds up as you scale.

This is today's list. It'll look different in a few years as models and infrastructure change. Naming your tradeoffs out loud is the part that lasts.

Sculley et al. · Hidden Technical Debt

In the diagram from this paper, the ML model is a tiny box surrounded by everything else.

The real cost is in that everything else: data pipelines, configuration, monitoring, glue code.

CACE — Changing Anything Changes Everything. Swap a model or tweak a prompt and behavior shifts in places you weren't looking.

These systems are quick to prototype and slow to keep running.

The takeaway

The model is maybe 10% of the work.

The other 90% is evals, monitoring, and system design. That's what we spend ten weeks on.

Yan · Patterns for LLM systems

Yan catalogs the moves people actually use to make these systems dependable enough to ship. A few we'll come back to:

Evals — how you know it's working (Week 3).
Retrieval — pull in real data so the model isn't making things up.
Guardrails — limit what it's allowed to output.
Caching — cuts both latency and cost.
User feedback — routes real signal back into the system.
Putting them together

Sculley tells you why these systems are hard and where the cost hides. Yan gives you the moves for dealing with it.

What this course adds: picking the right move for your situation, and showing evidence it actually helped.

Lab · after the break

The AI-fit exercise

In groups of four: place each product on the fit spectrum, and name the single most important tradeoff.

The fit spectrum
Strong fit
Rules can't do the job, and it's okay when the AI is occasionally wrong.
Depends on execution
Could go either way, depending on how tightly you scope it.
Overkill
There's already a correct answer you could just compute.
